Why availability

Today it is an advantage. Tomorrow it is the entry ticket.

Chains that reach 98–99.5% availability become the first place customers look. Below about 90%, availability alone creates a lead; when the leaders reach 98–99%, it becomes the level of play. The one who does it first wins.

+5 pp ≈ +20% five more points of availability came with about 20% more sales — margin grew faster still Recurring field observation · not a promise
35 → <20 days of inventory, with high availability maintained One project

Why nobody sees the loss. ERP and POS systems record sales, not the sales that did not happen. Without a daily history of availability for every product in every location, lost margin stays invisible.

The problem

Why is the product missing — and why is there too much of it?

Empty shelves and excess stock look like opposite problems. Often they come from the same place: the way your company makes decisions about goods — its rules, parameters, data and accountability.

Fix one shortage and it returns with another product. Change the process, and it stops returning.
